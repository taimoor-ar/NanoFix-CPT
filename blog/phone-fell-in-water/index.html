Phone Fell in Water? What to Do (and What Not to Do)

By NanoFix · Published 1 November 2026

You drop your phone in the sink, the pool, the sea or a drink, and your heart sinks with it. What you do in the next few minutes matters a lot. Some phones survive a dunking with no problems, and others don't. Acting quickly, and avoiding a few common mistakes, gives yours the best chance.

Short version: take it out, switch it off, don't charge it, dry the outside, and get it checked as soon as you can.

What to do right now

  1. Get it out of the water straight away. Every second in the liquid gives it more time to get inside.
  2. Switch it off and leave it off. Power running through a wet board is what causes most of the lasting damage. If it already turned itself off, don't turn it back on.
  3. Don't plug anything in. No charger, no cable, no headphones.
  4. Remove the case, the SIM tray and any cards. This lets moisture escape and helps a technician see what's going on.
  5. Wipe the outside with a dry, soft cloth. Gently, without pushing water into the ports.
  6. Keep it somewhere dry at room temperature. Lay it flat or stand it upright in open air, away from heat.
  7. Get it checked soon. The sooner a technician opens it up, the better the chances.

What not to do

  • Don't turn it on to "see if it still works". This is the most common way a phone that could have been saved is lost.
  • Don't charge it. A wet charging port plus power can short the board.
  • Don't use a hairdryer, oven, microwave or heater. Heat can damage the battery and internal parts, and can push moisture deeper.
  • Don't put it in a bag of rice. Rice doesn't reliably pull moisture out of the inside of a phone, and it can leave dust or starch in the ports.
  • Don't blow into the ports or use compressed air. It can push water further inside.
  • Don't shake it or press the buttons over and over. That moves the liquid around.

Does the type of liquid matter?

Yes. Fresh water is the least harmful. Salt water (the sea), pool water, and sugary or milky drinks like coffee, tea and cooldrinks are worse. They leave residue and cause corrosion faster. If it happened at the beach, tell the technician it was seawater so they know what to look for.

My phone is "water resistant". Am I safe?

Not necessarily. Water-resistance ratings are tested in fresh water under lab conditions. The seals can weaken as a phone ages, and after a screen or battery repair. Salt water, hot water and pressure from a fall can also get past them. If your phone got properly wet, it's worth having it checked even if it seems fine.

Problems that can show up later

A phone can seem fine at first and develop faults days later, as corrosion builds up inside. Watch for:

  • Fog or moisture behind the camera lens or screen
  • A crackly, muffled or silent speaker or microphone
  • Charging that stops or starts on its own
  • A flickering screen or lines on the display
  • A battery that drains much faster than before
  • A phone that won't turn on at all

Can a water-damaged phone be saved?

Sometimes, and we won't promise it. The outcome depends on how long it was wet, what liquid it was, and whether it was switched on or charged afterwards. What we can promise is an honest diagnosis.
